The survey

Questions are in English and Traditional Chinese. Ask about the past 30 days, not "would you", because people predict their own behaviour badly. Section D uses the demo at michellesrm.github.io/basket-radar.html.

Opening text for respondents

We are HKU students designing a grocery app for Hong Kong families. This anonymous survey takes about 10 minutes. There are no right answers, and you can stop at any time. We do not ask for your name. If you agree to a follow-up chat, your contact is used only to arrange it and is deleted after the project. 我們是港大學生，正在為香港家庭設計一個買餸應用程式。此匿名問卷約需10分鐘，答案沒有對錯，你可隨時停止。我們不會收集你的姓名；如你同意跟進訪談，聯絡方法只用作安排訪談，並會在項目完結後刪除。

Paste into Google Forms or Microsoft Forms, one question per item.

Interview guide · 15 minutes

The survey shows how many; conversations show why. Talk to at least five people from the target group: the main grocery shopper in a household of three or more. Talk to them at home, or just after a supermarket trip, with their most recent receipt in hand. Ask what they did, not what they would do.

  1. The last trip. "Show me your last supermarket receipt. Walk me through that trip: which store, why that one, what was on the list?" 「可唔可以畀我睇吓你上次超市嘅單？講吓嗰次點解揀嗰間、買咗乜。」
  2. Running out. "When did you last run out of something like milk or eggs? What happened?" 「上次牛奶或者雞蛋用晒係幾時？之後點做？」
  3. Prices. "How did you check prices last week? Show me on your phone." Watch whether they open the Consumer Council app, a chain app, or nothing. 「上星期你點格價？可唔可以喺手機示範？」
  4. Multi-buy. Show the Slimilk example (Wellcome HK$32 with "2 for $37"; ParknShop HK$22 with "Buy 2 save $8"). Ask which is cheaper for two packs, and watch how they work it out.
  5. Coupons and waste. "Open your yuu or MoneyBack app. Are there coupons you forgot about? Did anything expire in your fridge this month?"
  6. The demo. Hand them the demo on your phone. Say nothing for two minutes, and note where they tap, hesitate or smile.
  7. Receipts and money. "Would you photograph every receipt for this? What would make it worth it: points, cashback, or nothing?" "Would you pay HK$98 a year for the family list? Who in your family would use it?"
  8. Trust. "A brand pays us when you buy its product after seeing an offer. How do you feel about that?"
Note-taking rules. Write down exact words only when you heard them, and ask permission to quote. Record who you spoke to by role, household size and district, never by name. Write the surprise column on the same day, before you talk it over with the team.

Running it

Who to ask, and where

Target: at least 100 complete responses, with at least 60 from the main grocery shopper in a household of three or more. Single people still answer, but they are reported separately. Channels: housing estate and school parents' WhatsApp or Facebook groups, members' families, HKU staff and student groups, and posts on Threads and Xiaohongshu. Note which channel each response came from, because each one skews the sample differently: HKU contacts are younger, parents' groups more price-sensitive.

Timeline

  1. Pilot (2 days): five people complete it while thinking aloud. Fix any question they misread, and check it takes about 10 minutes.
  2. Field (7–10 days): share the link and track daily counts by channel. Start interviews alongside.
  3. Analyse (3 days): fill the surprise log below; update assumptions A4–A15 in the plan; write the "what surprised us" paragraph for section 3.2.

Google Forms setup

  • One form section per survey section. Branch after A1 and A3: a "No" to A1 ends the survey; non-shoppers skip section C.
  • Turn off email collection. G4 (contact for follow-up) is optional and kept in a separate form, so contact details never sit next to answers.
  • Shuffle the rows of D5 (feature usefulness) so the order doesn't bias the ratings.
  • E1–E4: number answers only, HK$0–2,000. Put E1–E4 on their own page, before E5 shows our price, so the price doesn't anchor them.
  • D-section tasks: open the demo link in a new tab and keep the questions visible.
Honesty rule from the spec. Invented quotes or survey results count as academic misconduct. Report the real n for every number. Don't drop answers you dislike. If fewer than 100 people respond, say so and report what you have.

Analysis and the surprise log

How to read the results

  • Report every number with its n. Example: "34% (n = 87 main shoppers)". Show the target group separately from everyone else.
  • Price questions (E1–E4): plot the four curves. The acceptable range runs from where "too cheap" crosses "getting expensive" to where "bargain" crosses "too expensive". Check whether HK$98 falls inside it.
  • Demo tasks (D1–D3): report how many answered correctly and the median ease score (1–7). Under 5.5 means the screen needs work.
  • Open answers: two team members code them separately into themes, then compare. Quote one or two answers per theme.
